Research basics

Primary vs. secondary research

For validating a new idea, primary research usually matters more.

PRIMARY

Data you collect yourself — surveys, interviews. Slower, but specific to your idea.

SECONDARY

Existing data from other sources — reports, studies. Faster, but may not fit your exact question.

Survey question design

Avoid these two traps

Good questions test real behavior: frequency of use, willingness to pay, current alternatives.

LEADING QUESTION

"Wouldn't a faster checkout process be great?" — pushes the respondent toward "yes."

VAGUE QUESTION

"Do you like this idea?" — doesn't test willingness-to-pay or actual behavior.

Hour 2 · Activity

Design Your Validation Survey

Goal: a short survey testing real demand, with a target of 30+ responses.

  1. 01 Write 5-8 questions testing willingness-to-pay, frequency, and current alternatives.
  2. 02 Check each question: is it leading? Is it vague?
  3. 03 Identify your direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the "do nothing" alternative.
  4. 04 Trade your draft survey with another team — they flag any leading/vague questions.
  5. 05 Finalize and prepare to distribute this week.
